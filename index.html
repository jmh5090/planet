01

Why the standard playbook returns nothing

Point a conventional AI competitive-intelligence workflow at Vantor and it comes back empty. Not because there's no signal — because Vantor has no G2 listing, runs no ad campaigns, and publishes no pricing page. The signal in this market is real, dense, and mostly filed with the federal government.

The digest

01

Google

Model release Platform

Google made the representation layer free. Any positioning that rests on model quality is now competing against zero.

Signal

AlphaEarth Foundations publishes annual 64-dimensional, int8-quantised embeddings for every 10 m cell on Earth, 2017–2024 — free in Earth Engine. In October 2025 Google added Geospatial Reasoning, a Gemini framework that chains Earth AI models (imagery, weather, population) to answer multi-step questions in natural language.

Battlecard line

“Google's models are excellent, and they're free — we use that layer too. What Google can't sell you is a picture of your site taken this morning. They have no satellites. Every image in Earth Engine is somebody else's, on somebody else's schedule.”

Detail & sources

What's actually new

Two distinct things, often conflated. AlphaEarth Foundations (DeepMind, July 2025) is a geospatial foundation model that fuses Sentinel-2 optical, Sentinel-1 radar, and Landsat thermal into a compact per-pixel embedding; published parameter estimates run 480M–1B. Earth AI + Geospatial Reasoning (October 2025) is the orchestration layer on top, open to trusted testers.

Where this cuts against us

The embeddings are annual and derived from public sensors — which is exactly why they're free, and exactly what bounds them. They describe what a place is over a year. They cannot describe what changed on Tuesday. That's the boundary of the free tier, and it's the boundary of Google's threat.

Where it genuinely hurts

For any customer whose question is annual and continental — land cover, crop type, deforestation baselines — the free stack is now sufficient. That is real revenue that no longer needs a commercial provider.

02

Vantor

Partnership Structural

Vantor is the only competitor running Planet's entire playbook — fleet, software, and government trust — and in April it started buying the one thing we call ours.

Signal

Three moves in seven months. October 2025: Maxar Intelligence rebrands as Vantor and launches Tensorglobe (Cortex for constellation tasking, Forge for sensor fusion, Nexus for secure hosting). February 2026: first spatial-intelligence company to deploy Google Earth AI models inside air-gapped government networks. April 2026: announces Vantage (20 cm) and Pulse (40 cm) — a fivefold increase in collection capacity, targeting revisit as often as every 15 minutes. Pulse launches from 2027, Vantage from 2029.

Battlecard line

“Vantor's Pulse constellation is a 2027 promise with 2029 follow-on. Our monitoring fleet has been imaging the whole landmass daily for years, and our archive goes back over a decade. Ask them what their imagery of your site looked like in 2019 — then ask us.”

Detail & sources

Correction worth carrying into the room

It is commonly said that Vantor gets its satellites through its sister company. That's backwards, and the direction matters. Vantor owns and operates the WorldView constellation directly — four legacy electro-optical satellites plus six WorldView Legion. Lanteris (formerly Maxar Space Systems) is the manufacturer, not the operator. Vantor is a vertically integrated fleet owner, which makes it a materially stronger competitor than the “software company that buys imagery” framing suggests.

Why the Google partnership is the sharper signal

Bigger than the constellation news. Vantor didn't build a rival foundation model — it took Google's and put it somewhere Google can't go alone. That is the emerging shape of this market: model quality from the hyperscaler, deployment and accreditation from the fleet owner. Planet needs a credible answer to “can this run in our SCIF.”

Where this cuts against us

The revisit gap is our position and it has a stated expiry. Pulse targets 15-minute revisit at 40 cm — better resolution than PlanetScope at frequencies approaching daily monitoring. If it ships on schedule, the “near-daily” argument weakens from 2027 and is contested by 2029.

03

Free public data

Structural Not a company

The free tier used to be pixels. It is now pixels plus a foundation model. Every year, more of the low end of our market stops being a market.

Signal

Sentinel-2, Sentinel-1 and Landsat are not just free inputs any more — they're the training substrate for AlphaEarth, which is itself free. Clay's Sentinel-2 embeddings are openly published on Source Cooperative. Copernicus Data Space added Landsat 8/9 in January 2026, consolidating the free stack under one roof. Meanwhile the FY2026 US budget request restructures Landsat Next, directing USGS and NASA to study lower-cost architectures.

Battlecard line

“If the answer can wait for the next Sentinel pass, use Sentinel — we'll say so. Buy from us when the question has a date on it: what changed since yesterday, what did this look like on the 14th, tell me within the hour. Free data is on a five-day orbit and it doesn't take requests.”

Detail & sources

Why this belongs on a battlecard at all

No competitor has a larger installed base. For a great many commercial use cases, “good enough and free” beats “better and paid” — and the gap narrowed sharply in 2025 when the free tier gained an analysis-ready embedding layer. Sales teams lose these deals without ever seeing a competitor's name on the evaluation.

The honest version

Conceding this ground fast is the stronger play. A rep who volunteers “Sentinel is genuinely fine for that” earns the right to be believed on the cases where it isn't. Fighting free data on annual land cover is a losing argument that also costs credibility.

Worth watching

Landsat Next restructuring is a live variable in both directions: any degradation in continuity of free data would expand the commercial market, and a scientific advisory panel has publicly urged the Interior Department to keep Landsat data free. Track it; don't build a deck around it yet.

Sources

  • USGS — Landsat Next mission page and FY2026 budget restructuring
  • Science / AAAS — Keep Landsat data free, panel urges Interior Dept.
  • Copernicus Data Space — Landsat 8/9 available in Copernicus Browser · Jan 2026
  • Source Cooperative — Clay Sentinel-2 embeddings, published openly
04

Esri

Platform Partnership

Esri isn't trying to win the model race — it's hosting it. Whoever's model wins, the analyst still opens ArcGIS.

Signal

At its 2026 User Conference Esri announced foundation models are becoming part of ArcGIS — hosting Prithvi, Dynamic-One-For-All and Clay while developing its own. Alongside: an Azure OpenAI integration putting 90+ pretrained deep-learning models in the GeoAI toolbox, and a strategic collaboration agreement with AWS on geospatial foundation models and training infrastructure.

Battlecard line

“Esri is where the analysis happens — we're not trying to replace that, we're trying to be the freshest layer in it. The question isn't Planet or ArcGIS. It's whether the imagery under your ArcGIS workflow is from this week or last quarter.”

Detail & sources

The threat is distribution, not capability

Esri's model-agnosticism is the strategy, not a hedge. By hosting every foundation model — including open ones like Clay — it makes the choice of model an implementation detail inside a workflow it owns. Model differentiation becomes a dropdown. Workflow lock-in doesn't.

Where this cuts against us

If ArcGIS becomes the default surface for geospatial AI, imagery providers are commodity inputs selected on price and freshness. Freshness is defensible; price is not. This is the clearest live example of the value-migration risk in section 03.

Sources

  • Esri ArcNews — GeoAI in the Age of Foundation Models · Spring 2026
  • Esri blog — What's new in AI tools and models in ArcGIS · Q2 2026
  • GIM International — Esri partners with AWS to advance GenAI in geospatial solutions
05

LGND

Funding Partnership

LGND is not the threat. The fact that $9M now buys a credible geospatial AI product is the threat.

Signal

LGND raised a $9M seed led by Javelin Venture Partners in July 2025, positioning as “ChatGPT for the Earth” — natural-language search over vector embeddings, targeting insurance, finance and public agencies. Founded by the team behind the open-source Clay model. In 2026 it partnered with CARTO and integrated AlphaEarth.

Battlecard line

“Natural-language search is table stakes now — a seed-stage team shipped it on free data. Ask what imagery it's searching. If it's public satellites, you're getting a very good interface onto a five-day refresh.”

Detail & sources

Read the funding number as a cost curve, not a competitor

$9M would not have bought a working geospatial AI product three years ago. It does now, because the substrate — imagery, embeddings, compute — is free or near-free. Expect more entrants at this size, and expect them to compete on interface quality, because that's the only layer left to build.

Where this cuts against us

Thin-layer companies move faster on UX than a satellite operator can, and they'll set customer expectations for what a geospatial interface feels like. Planet doesn't need to win that race — it needs to not lose the customer relationship to whoever does.

Sources

  • TechCrunch — LGND wants to make ChatGPT for the Earth · 10 Jul 2025
  • Everywhere Ventures — LGND raises $9M seed
  • Source Cooperative / Clay — open embeddings alongside AlphaEarth
03

Where this is heading

Three trajectories the signals above imply, and what each means for how Planet should position now — not in 2028.

0–12 months

The substrate goes free; the clock does not

AlphaEarth is free in Earth Engine. Clay is open on Source Cooperative. Esri hosts both. Within a year, nobody is paying for a representation of the Earth's surface — the embedding layer is a commodity with several credible free implementations.

Position nowStop selling “our AI.” Sell the observation cadence no embedding can synthesise. A model can tell you what a place looks like; only a fleet can tell you what it looked like this morning.
12–24 months

Vantor buys the clock

Pulse and Vantage are a direct, funded, publicly announced assault on temporal advantage — fivefold collection capacity, revisit as often as every 15 minutes — from the one competitor that already owns satellites and already owns the classified channel. First launches from 2027.

Position nowThe window in which “near-daily” is uncontested is roughly twenty-four months. Bank the argument that outlasts it: you can buy revisit rate; you cannot buy retroactive history. A constellation launched in 2027 has no 2019 imagery and never will. Make archive depth the headline, not a bullet.
12–24 months

The fight moves from whose model to whose pipe

Esri is embedding every foundation model into the workflow layer. Vantor got Google's models into air-gapped environments first. Neither is competing on model quality — both are competing on where the analysis happens and who is accredited to run it.

Position nowPresence in ArcGIS and credibility in sovereign, air-gapped and on-premise deployments are no longer integrations to schedule — they are the condition of staying in the value chain. On-orbit inference on Pelican-4 is the strongest counter-move on the board: it moves processing to a place no software company can reach.
Planet wins where the question has a date attached.

It's the cleanest qualification test available to a sales team. What changed since yesterday? What did this look like on the 14th? Tell me within the hour. Those are fleet questions — near-daily revisit, cloud resilience through repeated passes, tip-and-cue from monitoring to high-resolution tasking, a decade-deep archive. Broad and slow questions — annual land cover, regional trend lines — are not, and free Sentinel data will usually answer them well enough. Qualify on the date, not on the vertical.

The risk, stated plainly

If models commoditise and interfaces consolidate, value migrates up the stack and Planet becomes a capital-intensive data supplier feeding somebody else's product. Owning satellites is the moat and the exposure at once: it's the thing Google and LGND structurally cannot replicate, and it's the reason the cost base can't be cut when pricing pressure arrives. Every trajectory above is a variation on the same question — does Planet own the customer relationship, or the pixels underneath somebody else's?

Adding Esri was one line. Dropping LGND is one line. That's the point — the competitive set should be cheap enough to argue about weekly.

Method

An agent queries the sources in the config block, clusters what changed, and drafts one entry per competitor in signal / so-what / action form. A person reviews and edits every entry before anything publishes. The agent proposes; it does not decide. Script and README accompany this page.

Sourcing

Every claim here is drawn from public announcements, published research, government budget documents, or company filings, each dated in the expanded detail of its entry. Nothing is inferred from private conversation, and no non-public Planet information appears anywhere on this page.

Limitations

One issue, five competitors, one output format. Forward-looking statements about Vantor's constellation are that company's public schedule, not a forecast. The trajectory section is argued interpretation, not sourced fact, and is labelled as such. Contract-award coverage is limited to unclassified filings — the classified portion of this market is, by construction, invisible to any workflow like this one.
